NCAA RULE 1: PAY-FOR-PLAY PROHIBITION.
Compensation provided to a student-athlete for the use of their Name, Image, or Likeness (NIL) must be for work actually performed. 
Compensation may not be contingent on athletic performance or achievement. 
Explicitly prohibited clauses include payments conditioned on: scoring points, winning games, specific athletic statistics, or roster status.

NCAA RULE 2: INSTITUTIONAL INVOLVEMENT.
No university, athletic department, or staff member may directly compensate a current or prospective student-athlete for their NIL. 
No specific NIL deal can be guaranteed or promised by the institution as an inducement for enrollment.

NCAA RULE 3: PROHIBITED INDUSTRIES (VICES).
Student-athletes are prohibited from entering into NIL agreements with companies or brands associated with the following industries:
- Adult entertainment or pornography.
- Alcohol products (beer, wine, liquor).
- Tobacco and nicotine products (including vaping).
- Cannabis or controlled substances.
- Gambling, sports betting, or casinos.
- Performance-enhancing drugs (anabolic steroids).

NCAA RULE 4: DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS.
Student-athletes must disclose all NIL agreements to their educational institution's compliance office. 
Disclosure must occur within 7 business days of the effective date of the agreement. 
Failure to disclose may result in a loss of athletic eligibility.

NCAA RULE 5: PROFESSIONAL REPRESENTATION.
Student-athletes may obtain professional representation (agents or attorneys) for the specific purpose of securing and managing NIL deals. 
The agent must be licensed in the state where the educational institution is located. 
Athletes may not sign with an agent for future professional sports contract negotiations (NFL/NBA drafts) without jeopardizing amateur status.

NCAA RULE 6: CONFLICTS WITH TEAM CONTRACTS.
A student-athlete may not enter into an NIL agreement that conflicts with a team contract. 
For example, if the university has an exclusive contract with Nike, the athlete may not wear Adidas gear during team activities, practices, or games. 
Athletes may wear competitor gear during their own private time.
